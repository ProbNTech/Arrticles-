## Competitor gap insight

**Who ranks:** The top results for "handicap bathroom remodel cost" are:

- Angi (2026 data)
- HomeAdvisor
- Two Coohom articles
- DadImprovement (2025, includes funding)
- Kitchen & Bath Design Center (kbfdesign.net)
- Nationwide Builders (2026, contractor-finding angle)
- RemodelInspo (CAPS contractor directory)
- American Specialties (ADA guidelines for small bathrooms)

This is enough to count as a competitive SERP. We could only see titles and excerpts, not full pages.

**What they cover:** Almost all of them lead with a headline range (for example, $5,600–$12,000) and a feature-by-feature price list: grab bars, walk-in tubs or showers, doorways, and permits.

**What's missing or weak:**

1. **Matching cost to need.** They list features but don't help readers figure out which tier they actually need. Our angle: "price the person, not the bathroom," with three need-based levels.
2. **ADA confusion.** Snippets use "ADA-compliant" loosely. Few explain that ADA standards are a yardstick for private homes, not usually a legal requirement, or that strict compliance in a small room drives cost up.
3. **Hidden costs.** Curbless shower subfloor work, wall blocking, and surprises behind walls get little attention.
4. **Evidence for priorities.** None of the excerpts use injury data to show where to spend first, such as the CDC's toilet-area injury rates by age.

## Research log (each tag, the sources found or "unresolved")

**Overall cost, resolved.** Two domain-restricted searches:

- Angi: $5,600–$12,000. https://www.angi.com/articles/remodel-bathroom-handicap-accessible-cost.htm
- HomeAdvisor: $2,700–$16,000, average $8,400, plus the component table and the household-needs example. https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/additions-and-remodels/accessible-bathroom/

These sources overlap at the core, so both are presented as ranges.

**Labor $50–$150/hr: PARTIAL.** It appeared in an angi.com-restricted search summary but couldn't be pinned to a specific page.

**Grab bars, resolved.** HomeGuide gives $100–$350 per bar, bars at $20–$150, handyperson $50–$80/hr, plumber $75–$150/hr, and 2–4 bars typical. https://homeguide.com/costs/grab-bar-installation-cost

A second search found consistent figures ($150–$350 and $200–$350 per bar from other guides).

**Roll-in shower, resolved.** Angi gives $2,900–$6,600 (average $3,800), labor $400–$2,000, custom $4,200–$8,500, and kits $600–$3,200. https://www.angi.com/articles/cost-to-install-accessibility-shower.htm

A second, general search showed $3,000–$6,900 from another guide, which is consistent.

**Walk-in tub, resolved.** NCOA gives $3,000 to $17,000+ installed and installation of $1,000–$5,000. https://www.ncoa.org/article/how-much-do-walk-in-tubs-cost/

A second search (Angi accessible-bathroom summary: walk-in tubs $1,100–$17,000) agrees on the upper end.

**Doorway widening, resolved.** Angi gives $50–$3,000 (about $1,200 typical), costs by width, the structural engineer/header note, permits of $50–$300, and the hidden wiring/water damage warning. https://www.angi.com/articles/widen-doorway-cost.htm

A second search showed $700–$2,500 per door from another guide, which falls within range.

**ADA dimensions, resolved.** U.S. Access Board, Chapter 6 Toilet Rooms: 17–19 in. seat height, 42 in. side bar at 33–36 in., 60 in. circle or T-turn. https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-6-toilet-rooms/

Standards page: https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/design-standards/2010-stds/

**CDC injury data, resolved.** CDC MMWR 60(22), published 2011 (dated in text): toilet-area rate from 4.1 to 266.6 per 100,000 by age, and tub/shower highest overall. https://cdc.gov/mmwr/pdf/wk/mm6022.pdf
